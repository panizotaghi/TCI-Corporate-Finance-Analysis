## 2. The industry worldwide and in Iran

Telecommunications and internet is one of the most fundamental and strategic sectors of the global economy. It carries personal, business and government communication, and it also drives the development of other industries. As digital technology spreads, demand for communication services, data and broadband internet has grown sharply, and telecom companies have become strategically important worldwide.

### 2.1 Key features of the global telecom industry

Over recent decades the industry has moved from fixed-line telephony and basic mobile phones to 5G networks and the Internet of Things (IoT). Three major trends stand out:

- Heavy investment in next-generation infrastructure (5G, fiber optics, satellite)
- Mergers between large players to gain market share and cut costs
- Diversification into internet, digital TV, cloud and data-center services

### 2.2 Main global players

Several very large companies lead the market. They matter because of their revenue, and also because of their technological influence, scale and innovation:

1. **AT&T (USA)**: one of the largest telecom companies in the world, offering mobile, internet and TV services. It has also been active in media, through its acquisition of WarnerMedia.
2. **Verizon Communications (USA)**: a leader in 5G and a major provider of high-speed internet and mobile services in the US, with large investments in future-oriented infrastructure.
3. **China Mobile (China)**: the world's largest mobile operator by subscribers, with a central role in rolling out 5G across Asia.
4. **Deutsche Telekom (Germany)**: parent company of T-Mobile and one of the largest communication providers in Europe and the US. It is actively involved in building digital infrastructure.
5. **Vodafone Group (UK)**: a leader in Europe, Africa and Asia in mobile, broadband and IoT services.
6. **Orange S.A. (France)**: a large French operator present in many European and African countries, and one of the pioneers of 5G in Europe.
7. **NTT Group (Japan)**: one of Asia's largest telecom groups, active in mobile, internet, cloud and cybersecurity.

### 2.3 The telecom and internet industry in Iran

**Structure.** The Iranian telecom market is tightly regulated and controlled by the government. Private companies compete in some segments, but the main players are either state-owned or linked to quasi-governmental bodies. The key segments are fixed-line telephony, mobile, home and mobile internet, and data services.

**Main players:**

1. **Telecommunication Company of Iran (TCI)**: the most important player in fixed-line telephony and home internet infrastructure (ADSL, VDSL). It plays a major role in developing the country's telecom infrastructure and operates through many provincial companies.
2. **Hamrah-e Aval (MCI)**: the country's largest mobile operator and a subsidiary of TCI. It offers mobile, mobile internet, enterprise services and trial 5G. Its shares trade on the Tehran Stock Exchange.
3. **MTN Irancell**: the second-largest operator, launched in partnership with South Africa's MTN. It has led the rollout of new mobile internet generations (4G and trial 5G) and is active in data, IoT and new technologies.
4. **RighTel**: the third mobile operator, owned by the Social Security Organization. It focuses on data and tries to grow its market share through cheap internet packages and 4G support.
5. **Fixed internet providers (FCPs)**: companies such as Shatel, Asiatech, ParsOnline, HiWeb and Fanap Telecom, which provide home and business internet. Many of them work with the government under contracts to build out fiber-optic infrastructure.

## 3. Company overview

TCI is one of the largest and most important ICT companies in Iran and is considered the backbone of the country's communication infrastructure. It plays a key role in fixed-line telephony, broadband internet (ADSL and VDSL), long-distance and international services, and infrastructure communications.

### 3.1 History and structure

TCI stopped being fully state-owned in 2009 (1388), when 50% plus one share was sold to the Tose'e Etemad Mobin consortium as part of privatization. Even so, a significant stake is still held by the government, public non-governmental institutions and retail shareholders.

The company is organized as a holding. It includes the provincial telecom companies (in 30 provinces) and subsidiaries such as the Telecommunication Infrastructure Company, TCI International and Tose'e Ertebatat Mobin. Hamrah-e Aval (Mobile Communication Company of Iran) is the holding's main subsidiary.

### 3.2 Business lines

- Local and long-distance fixed-line telephony
- ADSL/VDSL fixed internet
- Fiber optics and high-speed enterprise internet (FTTx)
- Call-center, data-center and data transmission services
- Infrastructure services for other operators and government bodies
- Participation in the National Information Network project

### 3.3 Market position

TCI is the country's largest fixed-line provider, with a share of almost 100%. It is also one of the main players in fixed internet, with tens of millions of active subscribers. It owns the majority of Hamrah-e Aval, Iran's largest mobile operator.

TCI's revenue comes mainly from two sources:

- Fixed-line telephony and internet services, provided through the provincial companies
- Profit from its stake in Hamrah-e Aval

In recent years the company has faced rising network maintenance costs, ageing equipment, limits on tariff increases and falling fixed-line revenue. Projects such as fiber-to-the-home (FTTH) and faster internet services are its main efforts to modernize its revenue model and keep up with technology.

### 3.4 Mission and vision

TCI defines its mission as "providing sustainable, secure and inclusive telecommunication services to all segments of society". Its vision is to become "the leading digital infrastructure company in Iran". Achieving this requires investment in new technologies, tariff reform and better customer service.

### 3.5 TCI's position in the telecom value chain

The telecom value chain has several stages: infrastructure design and development, data transmission, distribution and service delivery, and interaction with end customers. Companies operate in different parts of this chain depending on their specialization. TCI sits in the middle-to-upstream part of the chain and plays a key role in several stages:

1. **Communication infrastructure (backbone and access layer)**: TCI builds, maintains and expands fiber-optic and copper networks, switches and exchanges. As Iran's main infrastructure builder, it provides the basic services that other operators and end customers rely on.
2. **Core communication services**: it directly provides fixed-line telephony, ADSL/VDSL internet, long-distance and international calls, and enterprise internet.
3. **Sales and customer service (retail and enterprise)**: through its wide network of provincial companies, it serves households, businesses and large organizations. This stage depends directly on customer experience, pricing and support.

In short, TCI is an **infrastructure-based operator**. It builds and maintains the infrastructure and also serves end users directly, so it is present in the infrastructure, service delivery and customer stages of the value chain.

### 3.6 SWOT analysis

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
| **Nationwide infrastructure ownership**: an extensive fiber network and exchanges across the country, a key competitive advantage | **Ageing infrastructure and equipment**: much of the network could not be upgraded because of sanctions, which raises maintenance costs |
| **High share of fixed-line and fixed internet**: near-monopoly in fixed-line and a large share of ADSL/VDSL | **Low revenue from fixed-line and legacy internet**: traditional revenue is falling as users move to mobile |
| **Stable income from Hamrah-e Aval**: the largest operator's annual profit is a major part of TCI's income | **Limited pricing power**: tariffs are set under government oversight and have barely risen for years, which squeezes profitability |

| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
| **Fiber-to-the-home (FTTH)**: the national home fiber project is a major chance to modernize the network and build stable revenue | **Competition from mobile internet operators** (such as Irancell), whose fast, affordable mobile internet takes share from fixed internet |
| **Growing demand for data**: remote work, online education and e-commerce keep expanding the market | **International sanctions and technology limits**, which restrict access to new equipment, technology and global suppliers |
| **Partnerships with startups and the private sector** to tap outside innovation | **Users moving from legacy to digital services** such as VoIP, messaging apps and mobile internet |
| **E-government and smart-city programs** (including IoT), which will need more communication infrastructure that TCI can supply | **Growth of more agile private FCPs** such as Shatel, Asiatech and HiWeb, with flexible, customer-focused business models |

## 4. Position against domestic and international competitors

### 4.1 Domestic market

The Iranian market has two main competitive segments: fixed-line telephony and fixed internet (ADSL/VDSL/FTTH), and mobile internet and communications.

**Fixed-line telephony and fixed internet.** TCI's main competitors here are private companies holding FCP (Full Communication Provider) licences:

- **Shatel**: ADSL and FTTH internet. A direct competitor in internet, but without a national fixed-line network.
- **Asiatech**: ADSL internet and enterprise services. A serious competitor in high-speed and enterprise services.
- **HiWeb**: fixed internet and fiber, focused on expanding internet in less-developed regions.
- **ParsOnline**: internet and data services, with a smaller share than Shatel and Asiatech.

TCI still holds a monopoly in fixed-line telephony, but it is losing share in fixed internet to more agile private operators.

**Mobile internet and communications.** The main players are:

- **Hamrah-e Aval** (a TCI subsidiary): the country's largest mobile operator
- **Irancell**: the second-largest operator, with investment from South Africa's MTN
- **RighTel**: the third operator, state-owned, with a smaller market share

Through Hamrah-e Aval, TCI leads the mobile market. However, since Hamrah-e Aval operates semi-independently, TCI's share of the mobile market shows up only indirectly in its financial statements.

### 4.2 International comparison

Comparing TCI with similar companies abroad helps show where it stands globally:

| Country | Company | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Turkey | Türk Telekom | Similar ownership structure and services, but more privatized and more profitable |
| India | BSNL (state) and Jio (private) | BSNL is in decline like TCI, while Jio became a leader through new technology |
| Germany | Deutsche Telekom | A telecom giant with global services and advanced technology |
| Saudi Arabia | STC (Saudi Telecom) | Far more advanced in 5G, digital and cloud services |

Compared with its global peers, TCI lags behind because of:

- No access to up-to-date equipment and technology (due to sanctions)
- Severe limits on foreign investment and competition
- Low tariffs and no pricing flexibility
- Ageing infrastructure and slow fiber rollout

### 4.3 Market share outlook

In my view, TCI's market share is declining for the following reasons:

1. **Falling fixed-line usage.** With mobile phones and messaging apps, demand for landlines has dropped sharply. In many homes the landline is no longer used.
2. **Rapid growth of mobile internet.** Irancell and Hamrah-e Aval offer 4G and 5G internet that is faster and more accessible than fixed internet, and many users have dropped ADSL.
3. **Strong competition from private FCPs.** Shatel, Asiatech and others are winning fixed-internet customers with more varied services, better support and innovative business models.
4. **Infrastructure problems and slow technology upgrades.** TCI still serves a large part of its customers over copper and old equipment, while the world is moving to fiber and gigabit internet.
5. **Heavy reliance on Hamrah-e Aval for profit.** TCI's profit depends heavily on Hamrah-e Aval, which means profitability in its core businesses (fixed-line and fixed internet) is low.

**Conclusion.** In the domestic market, TCI remains the strongest player in fixed-line telephony, but it is steadily losing share in fixed internet. Globally, it is behind similar companies in the region and the world, especially in technology and digital services. Its share of fixed internet is shrinking because of the fast growth of mobile internet, the better performance of private companies and delays in modernizing its network.

## 6. Products and operating revenue

TCI's main revenue-generating products and services are:

- **Telecom service usage fees**: periodic fees users pay for using phone lines and network facilities.
- **Telecom services (voice)**: revenue from landline calls (local, long-distance and international).
- **Telecom services (data)**: revenue from fixed internet (ADSL, VDSL, FTTH) and data transmission.
- **Other operating revenue: minimum monthly landline charge**: the minimum monthly amount charged to landline subscribers, even when usage is low or zero.
- **Maintenance and contracting**: revenue from maintenance contracts for equipment, exchanges and networks.
- **Access services**: fees for setting up or keeping a subscriber's access to the network (such as an internet port or phone line).
- **Power and space**: rent for providing power, space and infrastructure to operators and tech companies inside telecom exchanges.
- **Transmission line leasing**: revenue from other operators leasing or using transmission capacity (fiber or microwave links).
- **Facilities and services provided to Hamrah-e Aval**: revenue from providing infrastructure (space, power, links and so on) to Hamrah-e Aval, a TCI subsidiary.
- **Use of company facilities**: fees paid by companies or organizations for using TCI's equipment or technical facilities.
- **After-sales services**: support, installation, line repair and technical services for subscribers.
- **Other operating revenue**: anything not covered above, such as phone cards, digital add-on services or special projects.

## 9. Stock beta

Using the beta formula (all steps are in the Excel file), the results are:

| Ticker | Beta |
|---|---:|
| Akhaber (TCI) | 0.92 |
| Asiatech | 0.57 |
| Hamrah (MCI) | 1.54 |

A beta above 1 means the stock moves in the same direction as the market but more strongly, so it carries more risk than the market. Akhaber and Asiatech have betas below 1: they also move with the market, but less strongly, so investors can expect lower risk and lower returns than the market. Hamrah, with a beta above 1.5, can offer higher returns in exchange for higher risk.
