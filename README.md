# Corporate Finance Analysis: Telecommunication Company of Iran (TCI)

A fundamental and financial analysis of the **Telecommunication Company of Iran** (Tehran Stock Exchange ticker: **Akhaber**), prepared as a B.Sc. Corporate Finance course project. It combines an industry and strategy review with a spreadsheet model of the company's financial statements, ratios, valuation multiples, beta and break-even points. TCI is compared with two listed peers: **Hamrah-e Aval (MCI)** and **Asiatech**.

## Key findings

- **Growth**: operating revenue grew from 368 to 671 trillion IRR between FY1400 and FY1402 (about 35% a year in nominal terms).
- **Profitability**: operating margin rose from 7.6% to 12.8%, and net margin from 7.9% to 11.9%.
- **Leverage and liquidity**: liabilities are about 85% of total assets, and current assets cover less than half of current liabilities.
- **Leverage degrees (FY1402)**: DOL ≈ 2.05, DFL ≈ 1.62, DTL ≈ 3.32. A 1% change in revenue moves net profit by about 3.3%.
- **Valuation**: TCI trades at a higher P/E and P/S and much higher P/FCFE than its peers, driven by its low EPS. Its P/B is below 1.
- **Risk**: beta is 0.92 for TCI, 1.54 for Hamrah-e Aval and 0.57 for Asiatech.
- **Conclusion**: TCI keeps its monopoly in fixed-line telephony, but it is losing share in fixed internet to mobile operators and private providers. Much of its profit depends on its stake in Hamrah-e Aval. On the ratios and multiples studied, it is the least attractive investment of the three.

## Methods

- **DuPont / ROE**: net income ÷ total equity, tracked over three years
- **Beta**: estimated from one-year returns as β = (R<sub>stock</sub> − R<sub>f</sub>) ÷ (R<sub>market</sub> − R<sub>f</sub>), with a risk-free rate of 35% and a TSE index return of 48%
- **Degree of operating leverage**: % change in EBIT ÷ % change in revenue
- **Degree of financial leverage**: % change in net income ÷ % change in EBIT
- **Degree of total leverage**: DOL × DFL

## Data notes

- Amounts are nominal Iranian rials (IRR). The financial statements are in millions of IRR, and the valuation figures in billions of IRR.
- Fiscal years follow the Iranian calendar and end in March. For example, FY1402 ended in March 2024.
